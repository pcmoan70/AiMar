1 Summary 
English summary 
The aquaculture production of Atlantic salmon in Norway is diversifying and many different production 
systems, both at sea and on-land are currently being tested and evaluated. One of these systems is the 
new flow through laminar flow production pool developed by Andfjord Salmon AS. The system supplies 
a continuous flow of 100% seawater to the salmon, pumped from a depth of 45 m.  20 000 m3 of seawater 
are pumped into the pool per hour, ca. 333 m3/minute and estimated water exchange in the pool is 15-
17 times per 24-hour period. The rearing pool is 42 m long x 40 m wide with a mean water depth of ca. 
17.5 m. Water volume is ca. 29 400 ± 1500 m3. The production pool utilises laminar water flow via a 
pressure drop wall at the entrance to the pool. This leads to a turbulence free water flow through the 
pool and sediment falls to the pool substrate. Production in the pool started when the fish were stocked 
in the system on the 25th June 2022 and ended at slaughter between 29th June and 17th July 2023. 
Andfjord Salmon applied a very comprehensive welfare and health documentation plan for the first 
production cycle. This final report documents aspects of the welfare and health of the fish for the entire 
production cycle until slaughter, using a suite of input and outcome-based welfare indicators, in addition 
to a number of additional health indicators. 
 
A wide range of environmental OWIs and LABWIs documented mostly uniform high levels of key water 
quality parameters such as oxygen throughout the pool. Water current speeds, whilst low, did not 
manifest in poor water quality and may have been increased by the presence of fish in the system. Minor 
differences in measured water quality parameters were found at different locations in the system but 
should not have consequence for fish welfare and should allow for uniform distribution of fish in the 
system. As water is drawn from depth before being pumped into the system, the water temperature was 
somewhat more stable than what would be expected at corresponding depths in open cages and 
followed seasonal patterns in ambient water temperature. Although oxygen was added to the inlet water, 
an effect of water temperature and increased biomass on dissolved oxygen saturation was observed 
during most intensive parts of the production. The low natural water temperatures the fish were 
subjected to over the autumn through spring did not manifest in any welfare or performance risks, or 
risks for e.g., winter ulcers.  
Mortalities, excluding the sampling of fish without signs of disease were very low in the rearing system 
(ca. 2.5% for the entire documentation period) and any minor mortality events were closely monitored 
and remedial actions, where possible to identify and apply, were taken. Appetite was generally good 
throughout the production cycle and growth performance and condition factor were also good. Fish 
adapted to the rearing system and generally utilised the pool in a uniform manner. No fleeing/panic 
behaviours were observed at observation times around high and low water, and where potential fish 
sensitivity to ROV lighting in late October 2022 led to short-term panic in some fish and a short-term 
increase in associated mortalities, remedial action was taken to ensure this was short-lived and 
corrected for.  
The injury status of fish in the system was closely monitored during monthly manual audits of either i) 
100 fish (until 12th December 2022) or ii) 30 fish (numbers of sampled fish were reduced due to low 
winter temperatures from 26th January 2023 until 21st June 2023). The injury status of fish in relation to 
the Laksvel Operational Welfare Indicator (OWI) scoring system (Nilsson et al., 2022) has generally 
been good and the majority of injury based OWIs were scored as absent or mild for most of the 
production cycle. When OWI levels increased, this was noted and causes investigated. After smolt 
transfer several fish were sampled with poorer condition (emaciation state) and scale loss (<20% of fish 
sampled), but this injury status improved 90 days after stocking. Fish Health Services took circa monthly 
samples (minimum 10 fish per sample) from the heart, kidney, gill, skin, liver, spleen, pseudo branch,

pancreas, pyloric caeca and skeletal muscle (full organ pack, routine assessment) for histological 
examination to evaluate the integrity and quality of these organs. Skin samples were also taken for 
histological evaluation by Nofima. Histological evaluation of gill health and kidney status from the full 
organ pack assessment was considered to have high clinical importance and is summarized here with 
kind permission from Fish Health Services and Andfjord Salmon AS. Heart status is summarized based 
upon PCR data. Gill health was generally good at all timepoints, except for an increase in infectious 
pathogens that were the main cause of pathological findings from T6 to slaughter. Whereas skin got a 
higher score (more damaged) from the hatchery phase up until 60 days post-transfer to the pool. This 
skin health histology score was often higher than what was expected based upon the OWI scoring. 
However, it is not clear if this was due to reduced skin quality per se, poor sample quality, or to 
challenges and limitations with the sampling procedures. Histological findings in the skin were 
comparable to the welfare score at the start of the documentation period and included superficial 
damages such as lost epidermis and scale loss over the same time points, in the majority of analysed 
individuals. Based on these findings, skin morphology and skin health were followed more closely for 
the remainder of the production cycle. Fish developed Nephrocalcinosis in the hatchery phase before 
transfer to the Andfjord pool and we see the artefacts of this after their transfer to seawater. With regard 
to PCR based pathogen screening, between 80-100% of sampled fish from T0-T11 were categorized 
with suspected heart- and skeletal muscle inflammation (HSMI), with positive results for Piscine 
orthoreovirus 1 (PRV-1). At T4 and T5, 3% of sampled fish were positive for the parasite 
Paranucleospora theridion and at T6 43% of sampled fish were positive for the parasite Parvicapsula 
pseudobranchicola and 73% of the sampled fish positive for the bacteria Candidatus Branchiomonas 
cysticola. At timepoint T8, 17% of the samples were positive for the parasite Ichthyobodo salmonis. 
These pathogens persisted to the end of the production cycle but had no clinical impact on the overall 
health situation in the pool, which was classified by fish health services as either good or not requiring 
attention at all sampling points. The results for all other pathogen screenings were negative. Whilst only 
a negligible amount of Lepeophtheirus salmonis was observed in the system, there was a noticeable 
but relatively low abundance of Caligus elongatus during the late summer/autumn of 2022. This did not 
pose a major risk to fish health or welfare and was easily remedied by feed administered treatments. 
No Caligus elongatus were found in 2023. Infestation levels should nevertheless be monitored for future 
production cycles. 
Norsk sammendrag 
Det foregår en diversifisering av produksjonsteknologien som benyttes til oppdrett av laks i Norge og 
flere nye produksjonssystemer er under utprøving og evaluering, både på land og i sjø. Ett av disse nye 
systemene er utviklet av Andfjord Salmon AS og er et landbasert gjennomstrømmingssystem utformet 
for mest mulig laminære vannstrømmer i produksjonsenheten. Systemet tilfører laksen en kontinuerlig 
strøm av sjøvann som er pumpet opp fra 45 meters dybde. 20 000 m3 vann pumpes gjennom systemet 
per time, om lag 333 m3 per minutt, og den beregnede vannutskiftningen i bassenget er 15-17 
utskiftninger per døgn. Produksjonsenheten er et basseng som er 42 meter langt, 40 meter bredt, og 
ca. 17,5 meter dypt. Vannvolumet er omtrent 29 400 ± 1500 m3. Bassenget skal gi laminær vannstrøm 
ved at vannet føres gjennom en trykkfallsvegg ved bassenginnløpet. Dette skal gi en turbulensfri 
vannstrøm i bassenget som skal behjelpe sedimentering av partikulært stoff fra vannsøylen. Den første 
produksjonssyklusen i enheten begynte med et innsett av fisk den 25 juni, 2022, og sluttet med 
utslakting fra 29 juni til 17 juli, 2023. Andfjord Salmon iverksatte under produksjonssyklusen et 
omfattende dokumentasjonsprogram for fiskevelferd og -helse. Denne sluttrapporten dokumenterer 
aspekter ved fiskens helse og velferd under hele produksjonssyklusen, fra utsett til slakt, ved bruk av et 
bredt spekter av velferds- og helseindikatorer. 
Under produksjonssyklusen var vannkvaliteten jevnt over stabil og god. Målte vannhastigheter, om enn 
lave, medførte ikke dårlig vannkvalitet. Den romlige variasjonen i målte vannkvalitetsverdier var stort

sett forholdsvis lav og påvirker antagelig ikke fordelingen av fisk i bassenget. Vanntemperaturene følger 
sesongmessige variasjoner i sjøtemperaturene, men viser mindre grad av korttidsvariasjon grunnet det 
dype vanninntaket. Oksygen tilsettes i systemet, men økt biomasse påvirket metningsgradienten 
gjennom systemet utover produksjonssyklusen. De lave vanntemperaturene som ble observert gjennom 
vinteren og våren så ikke ut til å gi vesentlige velferdsutfordringer, ei heller vintersår. 
Dødeligheten var lav i systemet under den første produksjonssyklusen og om lag 2,5% av fisken som 
ble satt inn i systemet døde. Alle tilfeller av forøket dødelighet ble overvåket og fulgt opp med tiltak der 
dette var mulig og nødvendig. Appetitten til fisken og tilveksten var generelt god. Fisken så ut til å 
tilpasse seg produksjonsenheten og fordelte seg fint i bassenget. Ingen flukt- eller panikkatferd ble 
registrert i observasjonsperiodene, som foregikk rundt høy- og lavvann. 
Sår og skader på fisken ble fulgt opp gjennom omtrent månedlige besøk fra fiskehelsetjenesten hvor 
enten 100 fisk (frem til og med 12 desember, 2022) eller 30 fisk (fra og med 26 januar, 2023) ble

(Nilsson et al., 2022). I tiden etter at smolten først ble satt ut i bassenget ble det under prøveuttak

pylorusblindsekker, og muskler. Histologisk evaluering av gjellehelse og nyrestatus ble vurdert til å være 
spesielt viktig. Videre ble hjertehelse evaluert gjennom PCR-analyser. Gjellehelsen ble vurdert til å være 
god ved alle prøvetakingstidspunkt, men en økning av smittsomme patogener var hovedårsak til 
patologiske funn fra T6 frem til slakt. Skinnstatus, vurdert histologisk, var derimot dårligere i tiden etter 
at fisken ble satt ut i bassenget. Den histologiske vurderingen av skinnet indikerte en verre tilstand enn 
det man fikk inntrykk av fra velferdsskåringen, noe som kan skyldes håndteringen knyttet til 
prøvetakingen. Tilfeller av tapt epiderm og skjelltap ble særlig observert i tiden etter utsett og dette ble 
nøye overvåket i etterkant. Fisken hadde utviklet nefrokalsinose i ferskvannsfasen og virkninger av dette 
ble også observert etter overføring til Andfjord Salmon. PCR-basert patogenovervåkning viste at over 
80 % av prøvetatt fisk fra T0 til T11 kunne mistenkes å ha hjerte- og skjelettmuskelbetennelse (HSMB), 
med positive resultater for PRV-1. Ved T4 og T5 var 3 % av prøvetatt fisk positiv for parasitten 
Paranucleospora theridion, og ved T6 var 43 % av prøvetatt fisk positiv for parasitten Parvicapsula 
pseudobranchicola og 73 % av prøvetatt fisk positiv for bakterien Candidatus Branchiomonas cisticola. 
Ved T8 var 17 % av prøvetatt fisk positiv for parasitten Ichtyobodo salmonis. Disse patogenene vedvarte 
til produksjonssyklusens slutt, men lot ikke til å ha noen klinisk effekt på helsesituasjonen i bassenget. 
Fiskehelsetjenesten beskrev helsesituasjonen som jevnt over god under hele produksjonssyklusen. Kun 
begrensede mengder lakselus (Lepeophtheirus salmonis) ble observert på fisk i bassenget, mens det 
var merkbare mengder skottelus (Caligus elongatus) sommeren og høsten, 2022. Dette så ikke ut til å 
medføre negative effekter for fiskens helse og velferd og medikamentell behandling gjennom fôr så ut 
til å ha god effekt. Ingen skottelus ble observert i 2023.

2 Brief background 
Andfjord Salmon AS was founded in 2014 and in 2018 was granted a governmental concession for the 
land-based production of 10,000 metric tonnes of Atlantic salmon (Salmo salar L.) from post-smolt to 
slaughter. During the first production cycle, Andfjord Salmon operated a single flow-through land-based 
pilot pool (Pool K0) facility at Kvalnes, Andøya that supplied a continuous flow of 100% seawater to the 
salmon, pumped from a depth of 40 m. 20 000 m3 of seawater is pumped into the pool per hour, 
ca.  333 m3/minute and estimated water exchange in the pool is 15-17 times per 24-hour period. The 
rearing pool is 42 m long and 40 m wide with a mean water depth of ca. 17.5 m. Water volume is ca. 
29 400 ± 1500 m3.  The K0 production pool utilises laminar water flow via a pressure drop wall at the 
entrance to the pool leading to a turbulence free water flow through the pool. 
In 2019 Nofima AS and Andfjord Salmon AS established a collaboration to document aspects of the 
welfare and health of the fish produced in Andfjord Salmon’s new land-based farming pool concept from 
the time the fish were transferred to the system until slaughter.   
Andfjord Salmon applied a very comprehensive welfare and health documentation plan for the first 
production cycle. This final report outlines fish welfare, health and production data from the time of first 
stocking on the 25th of June 2022 until slaughter between 29th June and 17th July 2023 (ca. 12 months). 
The report utilises Welfare Indicators (WIs), the tools that are used to document the welfare status of 
fish. WIs can be either Input-based (primarily environmental WIs such as water quality indicators) or 
Outcome-based (indicators that are directly applicable to the animal at either the group or individual 
level). Farm-friendly indicators are termed Operational Welfare Indicators (OWIs) and WIs that are more 
complex and require further analysis e.g., at a laboratory are termed Laboratory-based Welfare 
Indicators (LABWIs), after Noble et al., (2018). In the Andfjord Salmon health and welfare documentation 
programme, partners used a combination of OWIs and LABWIs.

3 Aims and objectives 
Primary Objective: To document aspects of the welfare and health of Atlantic salmon raised in Andfjord 
Salmon’s new land-based farming pilot production system.  
Biological and environmental factors were documented throughout the production cycle from the time 
the fish were transferred to the production system until slaughter (although one water quality horizontal 
and vertical profiling was carried out before the fish enter the system). This report addresses the entire 
first production cycle from stocking in June 2022 until the final fish were slaughtered in the period late-
June – mid-July 2023. 
Sub-objectives include: 
i) 
Documenting environmental, input based LABWIs and OWIs (in partnership with 
Andfjord Salmon AS): oxygen, temperature, CO2, pH, TGP, turbidity, ammonia, 
nitrite, salinity and conductivity using data provided by Andfjord Salmon AS.  
ii) 
Horizontal and vertical profiling of the water quality (oxygen, temperature, pH, 
salinity, conductivity, turbidity) and water velocity in the system at three points 
before and during the production cycle. 
iii) 
Providing Andfjord Salmon AS with a simple OWI behavioural toolbox for 
documenting the behaviour of fish in their rearing system 
iv) 
Documenting group based OWIs: appetite, mortality (frequency and causes) and 
behaviour daily throughout the production cycle. 
v) 
Documenting individual morphological based OWIs on a ca. monthly basis using 
the range of OWIs outlined in the LAKSVEL OWI morphological scoring scheme 
(see Nilsson et al., 2022). Individual morphological based OWIs were also 
documented during the final stages of the hatchery phase. 
vi) 
Documenting monthly skin health status just prior to seawater transfer and 
thereafter monthly during the seawater production cycle using samples collected 
and provided to Nofima by Fish Health Services. 
vii) 
Integrate findings from fish health reports and sampling provided by fish health 
services and Andfjord Salmon AS (including gill histology, pathology and histology 
of abdominal organs, vaccine related pathology, nephrocalcinosis, clinical disease 
diagnosis, disease control screenings). This includes: a) an extended screening 
program (PCR analysis) for actual pathogens every month during the seawater 
production, and 1 screening at hatchery before seawater transfer (sampled by 
external Fish Health Services and analysed by an accredited laboratory), b) the 
counting of sea lice circa every month (carried out by Andfjord Salmon AS and 
external Fish Health Services), c) gill histology, pathology and histology of 
abdominal organs (sampled by external Fish Health Services and analysed by an 
accredited laboratory).

4.1 
Location 
This documentation study was undertaken at the Andfjord Salmon AS pilot production facility at Kvalnes, 
Andøya (locality 38897) at 69 °N (see Figure 1). The nearest other seawater salmon farm is Høgholmen 
(locality 10536), ca. 20 km southeast of Kvalnes. The nearest wellboat route is ca. 7 km from Kvalnes.

4.2 
Project plan  
The health and welfare documentation plan outlined in this report covers the reporting period from the 
time the fish were stocked in the Andfjord Salmon AS pilot production pool (hereafter termed “Pool K0”) 
from June 2022 until slaughter in late June to mid-July 2023, ca. twelve months following fish stocking.  
Within this plan, data was collected on: 
i. 
Water quality and water velocity profiling during 3 visits: i) one before smolt transfer on the 8th 
and 9th of June 2022 (Figure 2) and ii) two profiles with fish in the system (on the 14th and 15th 
of September 2022, without underwater lights and on the 13th and 14th of December 2022, with 
underwater lights, see section 4.9 below).    
ii. 
Daily data on real time water quality within the pool from time of stocking (see section 4.9) 
iii. 
Daily data on fish appetite, mortality and behaviour (see section 4.10) 
iv. 
Monthly data on morphological OWIs both before and after transfer to the pool and until 
slaughter (see section 4.10) 
v. 
Monthly data on fish health, including skin health parameters (see section 4.11).

4.3 
Fish stocking and history  
Fish were stocked in the Andfjord production pool K0 on the 25th of June 2022. Fish were produced by 
Nordland Akva AS, Åmøya locality (locality number: 26375) and were from a pooled hatchery Saga 
stock. Fish were collected from the hatchery on 24th of June 2022 by the Bjørg Pauline wellboat. Water 
temperature, salinity and dissolved oxygen (DO) levels in the hatchery at time of transfer were 8.0 °C, 
12 ppm and 95.8%, respectively. Fish were transferred in water with a temperature range of 8.5 to 
10.5 °C. Water temperature and DO levels in the Andfjord K0 pool at time of transfer were ca. 7.5 °C 
and 108% at 7 m (ca. half the depth of the pool), respectively. Average weight of fish at time of stocking 
was ca. 120 g and an estimated 198 883 fish were transferred into the pool. 
4.4 
Characteristics of the rearing system  
A detailed outline of the rearing pool design and system characteristics can be found in the Andfjord 
Salmon AS Biosikkerhetsplan drawn up by Åkerblå AS (and revised on 15.12.2022). 
In brief, the Andfjord Salmon land-based production pool K0 is flow-through and it supplies a continuous 
flow of 100% seawater to the salmon, pumped from 40 m depth. 20 000 m3 of seawater are pumped 
into the pool per hour, ca. 333 m3/minute and estimated water exchange in the pool is 15-17 times per 
24-hour period. The K0 pool is sunk into a concrete covered outer pool and consists of an internal 
composite tank/pool that is fixed to the substrate of the outer pool and has four walls. The rearing pool 
is 42 m long x 40 m wide with a mean water depth of ca. 17.5 m. Water volume is ca. 29 400 ± 1 500 m3.  
The K0 production pool utilises laminar water flow design via a pressure drop wall at the entrance to the 
pool. This is intended to produce a turbulence free water flow through the pool and sediment falls to the 
pool substrate (see section 4.7). For further details of the pool and system design see Figures 3 – 5 
below. To reduce the risk of fish colliding with the pool walls when jumping, eight grey floating air 
cushions (20 m long x 60 cm diameter, NorLense, Strønstad, Norway) were also mounted around the 
pool walls at the water surface during the first week of December 2022.

4.5 
Feeding system and regime  
An appetite-based feeding regime was applied in the Andfjord Salmon documentation project. Fish were 
surface fed using two different feeding approaches, i) from a single centralised Hexmodel rotor spreader 
(Akva Group, Bryne, Norway) which has an estimated feed spread of up to 20 m diameter, and ii) 
supplementary hand feeding all around the pool. Feeding was monitored using a mobile Gemini 
underwater camera (Imenco, Tysvær, Norway) during feeding. Feeding times varied with season and 
differed between the feeding methods (Table 1). From 25th June to 22nd August 2022, fish were given a 
supplementary hand feed to satiation during the whole day. After fish were introduced to the pool, they 
were fed a feed that was primarily based upon a RAS feed, as it is more stable in water and can be 
easier to collect and remove from the pool in order to maintain water quality. However, in mid-July 2022 
it was noted that the pellets sank relatively fast to the pool substrate and in collaboration with the feed 
producer, the sinking rate of the pellet was therefore reduced to give the fish a longer window to capture 
and consume pellets in the water column. In January 2023 it was noticed that some fish could collide 
with the feed delivery tube when they jumped, and the feed delivery tube was therefore padded to reduce 
risks from any collisions.

4.6 
Lighting system and regime  
The Andfjord Salmon production pool has no walls or roof over the pool. Fish were therefore subjected 
to natural seasonal changes in diurnal and nocturnal period and light intensity until 15th of November 
2022 when 24-hour artificial underwater lighting was applied. Fish were then held under 24-hour light 
until April 15th 2023. Underwater lighting was provided via four Aurora sub-led lights (Akva Group, Bryne, 
Norway) placed at 6 m depth, in a quad formation. Lights were ca. 10-15 m from each of the pool walls 
and positioned 20 m apart from each other (see Figure 9 below). 
4.7 
Pool cleaning and other husbandry routines 
The laminar water flow in the pool meant sediment fell to the pool substrate. The tank substrate was 
inspected and cleaned daily, and particulate sediment was collected using a custom Remotely Operated 
Vehicle, ROV (MEOX, Namsos, Norway) with the process taking 3 – 5 hours per day. The walls of the 
pool were cleaned every week in the summer and when needed in the autumn and winter using a custom 
ROV (MEOX, Namsos, Norway). It took approximately 1 hour to clean one of the tank walls. Additional 
cleaning was undertaken whenever the need arose. Mortalities were collected daily after feeding by an 
ROV with a mesh cage attached. When mortality collection was completed, the ROV was hoisted out of 
the pool. 
Due to an incident with mortality and stress caused by the ROV lights in October 2022 (see section 
5.2.1), Andfjord Salmon modified the unit so they could dim the strength of the lights and also be able 
to turn them off when the daily operations were concluded. They also updated the daily routines with an 
extra check to make sure this was done. 
4.8 
Fish welfare documentation 
Andfjord Salmon’s land-based seawater flow though rearing system is a novel production system for 
rearing Atlantic salmon on land until slaughter using flow through seawater. The FHF funded project 
“FISHWELL: Kunnskapssammenstilling om fiskevelferd for laks og regnbueørret i oppdrett” (Noble et 
al., 2018) has published an OWI handbook that includes a summary of various OWI toolboxes for 7 
different rearing systems for Atlantic salmon e.g., hatchery-based flow through systems, recirculating 
aquaculture systems (RASs), net cages, submersible cages, semi-closed containment systems in the 
sea (S-CCS) (see Kolarevic, Stien et al., 2018, in Noble et al., 2018). However, the FISHWELL 
handbook does not have a specific OWI toolbox for the land-based production of Atlantic salmon for 
either the Andfjord Salmon AS laminar flow pool concept or similar. Following discussions between 
Nofima and Andfjord Salmon in Autumn 2019 it was agreed that the most suitable OWI toolbox for 
monitoring the welfare of Atlantic salmon in the Andfjord Salmon pilot concept is to adapt the existing 
S-CCS OWI toolbox (see Figure 6 below).

4.10.2 
Individual level Outcome based OWIs 
To audit the injury status of fish within the Andfjord pool, morphological OWIs were recorded on 100 fish 
at the end of the hatchery phase (7th June 2022, ca. 2.5 weeks before smolt transfer) and ca. monthly 
following smolt transfer until 12th December 2022 according to the Laksvel OWI scoring protocol for 
Atlantic salmon (see Nilsson et al., 2022). A decision was taken after 12th December 2022 (primarily due 
to low winter temperatures) to sample 30 fish per month from 26th January 2023 until 21st June 2023. 
Laksvel standardises a range of morphological OWIs into a 0-3 scoring scheme where: 0 = undamaged, 
1 = minor injury, 2 = clear moderate injury, 3 = severe injury (see Nilsson et al., 2022 for a full overview 
and a pictorial scoring guide). Laksvel scores 14 morphological OWIs based upon: i) first impression, ii) 
vertebral deformities, iii) emaciation state, iv) maturation state, v) scale loss, vi) skin haemorrhaging, vii) 
lesions/wounds, viii) snout damage, ix) jaw deformities, x) eye opacity xi) eye damage, xii) opercular 
damage, xiii) gill status, xiv) fin damage and all 14 indicators have been included in the Andfjord Salmon 
documentation programme.  
4.11 Fish health documentation – health-based LABWIs  
Aspects of fish health status in the Andfjord K0 pool were also documented with a range of health based 
LABWIs applied at the individual level. These LABWIs included skin health, gill health, nephrocalcinosis 
status and pathogen screening. Skin, gill and kidney samples were collected from fish monthly, see 
Figure 2, from the time of stocking to document any potential changes in health status. Gill and kidney 
samples were sent to Patogen AS for pathogen screening (RNAlater fixed, n=30) and histopathology 
(formalin fixed, n=10) and skin samples (formalin fixed, n=10) were sent to Nofima for histological 
scoring. Nephrocalcinosis was also visually examined during routine monthly sampling using 
STONEHUNT, a 0-5 auditing scheme kindly provided by Åkerblå AS to Andfjord Salmon AS. Results 
were provided monthly by Patogen AS. All skin samples were sent to the Norwegian Veterinary Institute 
in Harstad for processing of histological sections; 2.5 μm with stained with AB-PAS and scanned (20x) 
for scoring (ImageScope). Histological scoring was manually conducted on all slides (slide software: 
Aperio Image Scope, Leica), using a 5-point scoring system (Karlsen et al., 2021) of epidermis quality, 
scale loss, mucus cells and melanin.

4.13 Auditing slaughter quality   
The primary quality characteristics of the fish were assessed upon slaughter in accordance with the 
NBS 10-01 standard by Nordlaks AS processing plant staff. This standard sets criteria for classifying 
the fish as either Superior, Ordinary, or Production grade, where a fish classified as Superior is nearly 
without fault. Per fish, a principal cause, if present, for downgrading it to either Ordinary or Production 
grade was recorded. Fish were slaughtered on June 30th and July 5th, 7th, and 17th, 2023, and a total of 
14 quality assessments were done on whole fish, each done on 100 randomly sampled fish, were made 
on those days by 4 different assessors, one for each assessment. For this report, the proportion of fish 
downgraded according to a specific principal cause was calculated and visualised. Furthermore, the 
quality grade of each slaughtered fish was evaluated by Nordlaks AS processing plant staff prior to 
packing. We here report the proportion of Superior grade fish delivered. 
4.14 Data management, auditing and analysis  
Production data was entered, organised, and stored in the Mercatus Farmer (Scale AQ, Trondheim, 
Norway) aquaculture data management software by Andfjord Salmon staff. Real time sensor data was 
fed automatically into the Clarify (Clarify, Trondheim, Norway) industrial data management platform. 
Water quality control measurements, OWI, and behavioural scores were entered and stored in Microsoft 
Excel (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA) spreadsheets. Water velocity recordings were post-
processed in version 1.5 of the software ExploreV Pro (Nortek, Rud, Norway), then further processed 
and visualised using the NumPy (Harris et al., 2020) and Matplotlib (Hunter, 2007) libraries for the 
Python programming language. The velocity profiles were interpolated and visualised as colour contours 
on a rectilinear, triangulated grid. One missing value, recorded near the inlet on September 14th, 2022, 
at 15 m depth and 20 m from the Eastern wall, was imputed using the K-nearest neighbours algorithm 
in the Scikit-learn library (Pedregosa et al., 2011). Data on fish production performance, as described in 
section 4.12, as well as fish mortality, feed delivery, and water quality were processed and visualised 
using the NumPy (Harris et al., 2020), Pandas (McKinney, 2010), Matplotlib (Hunter, 2007) and Seaborn 
(Waskom, 2021) libraries.

For reference, during the T-1 sampling prior to seawater transfer at the hatchery (see Figure 34A) nearly 
all fish sampled exhibited a Laksvel indicator score 0 for the majority of Laksvel morphological indicators, 
although all fish had a fin status of 1 (minor damage). Within one week of smolt transfer (T0) the majority 
of Laksvel OWI injuries were scored 0 or 1, meaning injuries were either mild or absent. However, there 
was an increase in the numbers of fish with mild/moderate emaciation (ca. 10% of fish had a moderate 
emaciation state), scale loss and eye opacity (ca. 40% of fish had moderate eye opacity and one had 
severe opacity) and ca. 15% of fish had moderate fin damage. This can be an artefact of both the 
physical transfer process and also adaptation of the fish after seawater transfer. Fish Health Services 
classified the welfare status of the fish as generally good and the moderate emaciation state of 10% of 
the sampled fish as normal following smolt transfer. 
The T1, T2 and T3 samples were carried out ca. one, two and three months after smolt transfer (late 
July 2022, late August 2022 and late September 2022, respectively) and a high portion of Laksvel OWI 
injuries were scored 0 or 1, meaning injuries were either mild or absent. There was generally an increase 
in the frequency of fish scored 0 at T1 compared to T0 (see Figure 34B). The portion of fish at T1 with 
mild and moderate eye opacity improved following smolt transfer although one fish again had severe 
opacity. At T2, the portion of fish with no or mild eye opacity was similar to T1 although ca. 10% had 
severe opacity. At T3, the portion of fish with mild and moderate eye opacity improved compared to T2 
and no fish exhibited severe eye opacity. Approximately 15% of fish had a moderate emaciation state 
and a further ca. 15% of fish exhibited a severe emaciation state at T1. Fish Health services suggested 
this was due to a high number of moribund fish being netted during sampling and that this figure was 
over-representative for the population as a whole. At T2, the number of fish with a severe emaciation 
state was lower than at T1, and at T3 only 1 fish in the sample exhibited a severe emaciation state.  At 
T1, approximately 40% of fish exhibited moderate scale loss and >10% severe scale loss. This trend 
can be somewhat normal following smolt transfer, and it can also be skewed by the over-representation 
of moribund fish in the sample. At T2, the number of fish with scale loss was lower than at T1 and 
approximately 40% of fish exhibited mild skin haemorrhaging and eye damage and ca. 50% had mild 
gill damage. At T3, approximately 40% of fish again exhibited mild skin haemorrhaging and eye damage 
was much reduced compared to T2, whilst ca. 50% again had mild gill damage. At T3 there was 
generally a marked increase in the frequency of fish scored 0 compared to T2, and the number of fish 
exhibiting OWIs classified as severe was much lower than at T2. Fish Health Services classified the 
welfare status of the fish at T1, T2 and T3 as generally good.

One fish was scored with severe eye opacity at T6 and T7, in addition to two fish with severe gill damage 
at T7. By T8 both the number of fish with moderate and severe eye opacity decreased, and no fish 
exhibited a Laksvel OWI level 3. Fish Health Services again classified the welfare status of the fish at

The T9, T10 and T11 samples were carried out ca. 10, 11 and 12 months into the production cycle, at 
the end of April, May and June 2023, respectively (see Figure 34C). The June 2023 OWI audit occurred 
ca. one week prior to the start of slaughtering. As with the greater part of the production cycle, the 
majority of Laksvel OWI injuries were again scored 0 or 1, meaning they were either mild or absent 
between T9-T11. At T9, very few fish exhibited any moderate injuries for any of the Laksvel OWIs 
audited. At T10 in May 2023, one level 3 emaciated fish was sampled in addition to two fish with severe 
eye opacity. At T11, just prior to slaughter, no fish exhibited level 3 (severe) injuries and the number of 
moderate injuries was generally low with the exception of eye opacity which once again increased the 
frequency in the sample. Fish Health Services again classified the welfare status of the fish as good for 
T9, T10 and T11.

5.3.1 
Skin histology  
Scoring of skin histology was carried out from T-1 to T11, and included an evaluation of skin features, 
such as parameters for skin integrity and quality, including scoring of the epidermis surface, scale loss, 
mucus cells and melanin. Many of the samples were of poor quality with score 2 or higher, indicating 
moderate or major damage. Score 3 or 4 indicates damage to the epidermis that will impact other score-
criteria, and these samples could not be used in further epidermis analyses (Figure 35 showing % of 
fish scored for epidermis quality). It is not possible to conclude if the quality of the samples is due to 
poor skin quality per se, or challenges and limitations with the sampling procedures. No samples from 
T3, T7, and T10 could be scored for mucus cells due to a damaged epidermis. As stated at the start of 
the morphological OWI section, fish were crowded, hand netted out of the water, and euthanized. This 
procedure occurred on a platform at the side of the pool and fish were then transported to a laboratory 
and sometimes left exposed to air before skin histology samples were taken. Potential challenges 
associated with sampling can include fish coming into contact with each other and the netting materials 
in the crowding/hand net, the fish transfer procedure to the lice counting tanks and transport buckets, or 
extended air exposure after euthanasia etc. In addition, air temperature was often low and variable 
between sampling points.

6 Discussion 
As stated earlier in this report, in many cases knowledge on how to select and apply appropriate, fit for 
purpose OWI and LABWI toolboxes for documenting aspects of fish health and welfare in new and 
emerging aquaculture systems is currently lacking. An aim of the Andfjord Salmon documentation 
project was therefore to use a wide-ranging and comprehensive suite of OWIs/LABWIs to document the 
possible effects of the rearing system and its operations upon aspects of the welfare and health of the 
fish it produces. 
6.1 
Fish welfare documentation  
6.1.1 
Input-based water velocity and water quality OWIs  
The Andfjord Salmon system design anticipated low water velocities in the range of ~1 cm/s that could 
potentially affect swimming behaviour, distribution of the fish and water quality in the system. In order 
to address those concerns, an extensive profiling of water velocity both vertically and horizontally was 
carried out in the system without and with fish. In the system without fish, average velocity for three 
profiles were in the range of expected, ca. 1 cm/s. The lowest average velocities were recorded in the 
middle of the system, furthest from the potential positive effect of inlet and outlet formations. The highest 
velocity measurements were reported close to the location of the two outlets (10 and 30 m from the 
eastern reference wall of the pool) that likely created vortexes extending at least 3 m into the system 
where measurements were done. At those points, velocity was almost 3 times higher compared to other 
measurements in the system. Similar observations are made for large commercial circular and octagonal 
tanks where peak velocities were related to central bottom drains in the tanks (Gorle et al, 2018). 
Interestingly, the water velocity in the system was not reduced by introduction of fish to the system. On 
the contrary, the average velocities (averaged cross-sectionally) had increased by two to six times in 
both profiling events completed after the introduction of fish. Again, the peak velocities were related to 
the vicinity of the system outlet and at greater depths. There were also one or two ROV observations 
during regular cleaning and inspection of the system of fish seeking out potentially higher water 
velocities near the tank outlets during autumn 2022 and these were not observed afterwards. Since 
then, fish started to form loose schools (see behavioural section below) and two distinct schools were 
often observed swimming in opposite direction from each other in the system (C. Thomassen personal 
communication). One of the potential reasons for different velocities between two samplings was that 
during measurements in the system without fish, the pumps were running at 90% capacity. This could 
have led to slightly lower measured water velocities. The measurements were done at slightly different 
time points relative to the lunar cycle and differing ocean water levels may have affected velocities within 
the tank, and this could possibly have been exacerbated by varying water current characteristics near 
the Andfjord water intake. Furthermore, experimental evidence suggests a potential for fish schools to 
increase water velocities around them (Park et al., 2022) and it was also noted that fish began to 
consistently form loose schools from late October 2022 (see Figure 30). 
Dissolved oxygen saturation, when measured at all 20 points within system, with and without fish, was 
above 85%. Minor stratifications in oxygen levels were observed close to the outlet in the system without 
fish, where the lowest values were recorded closest to the surface and the highest at 15 m depth. This 
could have been correlated with the minor temperature stratification at the same locations where the 
highest temperatures were measured at 1 m depth. In the second water profiling event in September 
2022, we recorded lower average dissolved oxygen saturation 3 m from the tank outlet (92.1%) 
compared to measurements done the day earlier at the same time 3 m from the inlet chamber (97.0%). 
This was most likely a consequence of the sharp temperature drop (3 °C difference in average 
temperature) from one day to the other. Considering the temperature ranges recorded during profiling 
and daily averages of real-time measurements, oxygen values were always within limits corresponding 
to a low risk of decreased fish welfare, as indicated by Remen et al., (2016) and Nilsson et al., (2022). 
 
58 
 
The availability of real-time oxygen measurements allowed for an improved and timelier overview of the 
environmental conditions the fish were exposed to during production. A closer look at the daily average 
oxygen saturations showed correspondence between inlet values and values recorded in the system 
and outlet chamber during first two months of the production. This trend was well aligned with the 
increase in temperature that occurred over the same time period. It was also clear that the difference 
between inlet oxygen and oxygen recorded in the system increased somewhat as biomass increased. 
From September until the end of October 2022, recorded values at all locations were more uniform. 
During this period, oxygen saturation stabilised in the inlet and as the temperature continued to decrease 
together with feeding delivery, this has affected oxygen use and with it the measured values in the 
system. For the remainder of the production period, dissolved oxygen saturation levels somewhat 
decreased whilst mostly remaining above 80%. There was also a tendency toward greater spatial 
variation in saturation levels and a greater divergence between inlet and outlet saturation levels. This 
follows from the increased oxygen demand within the system due to an increasing biomass metabolising 
a higher amount of feed over time. 
The total gas pressure levels measured in the inlet chamber were generally below 100%. However, the 
control measurements indicated incidences of total gas supersaturation, with levels up to 107.5%, both 
in July and August 2022, and in April 2023. To what extent this has affected the fish is unclear. Fish 
exposed to gas supersaturated water may develop emphysemas in tissues and emboli in the blood, a 
condition known as gas bubble disease which easily becomes fatal. Clear threshold levels for Atlantic 
salmon in seawater are seemingly not available, but symptoms and mortalities have been seen in wild 
smolts from total dissolved gas levels of about 108% (Stenberg et al., 2022). The depth of the Andfjord 
production tank gives the fish a possibility to compensate for surface gas supersaturation by staying low 
in the water column (Weitkamp and Katz, 1980), but the extent to which they would do this is unclear. 
As total gas supersaturation meters may need a long time to equilibrate and perform good readings, 
erroneous measurements or calibration cannot be ruled out. 
During the first production cycle, the average daily temperature in the system was between 4 and 12 °C. 
From late October 2022, and through to just prior to harvest, the temperatures were below 7 °C and 
corresponded to levels nearly outside (yellow code in Laksvel) or just outside (orange code in Laksvel) 
of the optimal range for post-smolt salmon (Nilsson et al., 2022). The experienced temperature range in 
itself doesn’t indicate that salmon’s welfare is reduced, but that the risk for welfare issues such as winter 
ulcer, is increased (Nilsson et al., 2022). Temperature profiling of the system with and without fish 
showed low variation between all 20 points measurements. The temperature preference of Atlantic 
salmon has been documented as a driver for its behaviour and distribution (Oppedal et al., 2011). The 
uniform temperature in the system allows for uniform distribution of salmon in the system which is what 
has been reported during a large part of the production cycle. Associations between daily average 
temperatures, oxygen saturation and feed delivery are discussed above. A sharp, intermittent drop in 
temperature of about 2.7 °C occurred during mid-September 2022 as seasonal temperatures started to 
decrease. The driver for this is unknown, but as it was picked up by multiple sensors and also during 
water quality profiling carried out by Nofima, it could not have been due to sensor malfunction. In the 
future, it would be interesting to consider historical ambient environmental data (if available) to see if 
similar drops are observed seasonally over the years to reduce potential risks for welfare at those 
timepoints.  
The salinity measured during system profiling prior to fish arrival was between 33 ppt and 34 ppt. This 
salinity can potentially be an osmotic challenge for newly smoltified salmon that prefer brackish water 
(Nilsson et al., 2022). This could be one of the reasons for a mortality score of almost 2 (Figure 18) for 
the weeks directly after transfer of smolts to the system, and is not specifically system related but can 
be a potential indicator of asynchronous seawater adaptation in smolts (that can be a problem for all 
seawater production systems). However, as stated in the group-based OWI section below, this temporal 
 
59 
 
pattern for generally higher post-seawater transfer mortalities is common in aquaculture and can be 
driven by numerous factors. In September 2022, a salinity of 32 ppt was recorded in for the inlet area, 
while the day after salinity in the outlet area was 34 ppt. The change in salinity, temperature and oxygen 
that happened from September 14th to September 15th 2022 can indicate mixing of water in the deep 
seawater layers from which the water is pumped into the system. Realtime salinity data were not 
available for this period. Beyond this, both salinity and conductivity remained relatively stable through 
the different time points. 
A survey of large tanks operated at Norwegian commercial smolt and post-smolt sites (Summerfelt et 
al., 2016) showed that the hydraulic retention time (HRT) in tanks in Norway has been reduced from 67-
171 minutes since 2013 to 35-50 minutes in the tanks on land regardless of the choice of production 
system. Low water flow and longer hydraulic retention times in general can increase the risk for 
accumulation of carbon dioxide and ammonia in the system as the biomass grows and feed consumption 
increases; this was one of the focal points of the documentation programme at Andfjord Salmon. 
Realtime data for CO2 were available from a Seaguard mounted sensor from mid-August 2022. These 
data are highly variable in time but indicate the effect of increasing biomass on the increase in CO2 
closer to outlet while inlet concentrations remained stable throughout the production period. Control 
measurements performed by Andfjord personnel plateau at about 2 mg/L towards the end of the 
production cycle, which is within acceptable limits for Atlantic salmon. The results of CO2 monitoring 
during production show that the HRT of 90 minutes in the pool was sufficient to keep low concentrations 
of this metabolite even at highest standing biomass. Recent studies carried out in RAS have shown that 
CO2 concentrations over 10 mg/L can affect the growth of Atlantic salmon parr (Khan et al., 2018; Mota 
et al., 2019). There is nevertheless a paucity of information on the effects of elevated CO2 concentrations 
on post-smolts during the seawater stage. The pH in the inlet water was relatively stable throughout 
production. Daily average pH values do show steady increase from 8.3 to levels approaching 8.8 which 
can also be a consequence of sensor ageing or drift over time in cases when manual calibration is not 
possible. The inlet pH control measurements, albeit few in number, indicated a relatively stable pH at 
levels just above 8. Contrary to the inlet measurements, the pH levels recorded by the Seaguard system 
within the pool showed considerable oscillation in the recorded pH values over time. The control 
measurements in the pool, taken during the spring of 2023, showed less variation, with levels ranging 
between 7.5 and 7.8. These, accompanied by, the pH measurements done during the water quality 
profiling events in 2022 indicate a tendency toward acidification in the pool. This is expected due to the 
increasing CO2 levels in the tank. Nevertheless, the pH levels seemingly remained within the 7-8.5 
tolerance range suggested for Atlantic salmon post-smolts (EFSA, 2008).  
Turbidity values during the three profiling events that are outlined in this report were almost 
undetectable. One of the reasons could be the fact that we tried to avoid any potential effects of feeding, 
salmon behaviour and sludge collection on the water quality measurements (e.g., these processes were 
paused during sampling). However, the control measurements done by Andfjord personnel were also 
very low and stable throughout the production cycle. There is not enough available information to 
suggest safe operating thresholds with regards to turbidity in Atlantic salmon aquaculture. To the best 
of our knowledge, the negligible turbidity levels recorded in the Andfjord system are unlikely to indicate 
a risk of adverse fish welfare states. The real time measurements of turbidity from the Seaguard 1 
system were initially unrealistically high, but then fell to values seemingly corresponding to the control 
measurements toward the end of 2022, remaining so for the rest of the production cycle. Measurements 
of turbidity in real-time can be very demanding as the presence of light and larger particles can lead to 
inaccurate measurements. Fouling on the sensor surface is an additional challenge and regular 
maintenance of probes are necessary for trustworthy recordings. 
The total ammonia nitrogen levels measured in the pool by Andfjord staff seemingly increased 
throughout the production cycle, which is expected due to an increased biomass and feed load resulting 
 
60 
 
in an increase in ammonia excreted within the system, The concentrations, roughly corresponding to a 
maximal NH3-N concentration slightly above 0.01 mg/L, are ten times lower than the levels that are 
known to affect the fish physiologically (Knoph, 1996, Alabaster et al., 1979). The nitrite concentration 
levels, while mostly remaining below the appropriate measuring range of the applied instrument, 
increased slightly during the spring of 2023. This may indicate some capacity for, e.g., microbial 
ammonia oxidation within the system. The nitrite levels seen in the Andfjord system are not known to 
cause adverse effects on Atlantic salmon welfare. The high chlorinity of seawater also protects against 
the uptake of nitrite in the blood (Gutierrez et al., 2019). 
6.1.2 
Group level Outcome based OWIs  
The monthly mortality rates of the Andfjord Salmon system were low compared with the median mortality 
rates for the geographical production zone in which the Andfjord Salmon system is located, denoted PO 
10, and the adjacent zone, denoted PO 9 for the years 2021 to 2023 (Sommerset et al., 2024). The 
temporal pattern in which a relatively higher proportion of mortalities is seen following transfer from a 
freshwater hatchery to seawater is common. The ultimate causes for this can be related to e.g., poor 
seawater tolerance due to improper smoltification, vaccination side effects, mechanical damage from 
pumping and handling during grading, vaccination, or delivery during the later stage of the hatchery 
phase, and adverse or stressful conditions during wellboat transport (e.g., Sommerset et al., 2022, 
Iversen et al., 2005, Mutoloki et al., 2004). 
Most mortalities in the Andfjord Salmon system seemed to have been due to trauma (injuries) with an 
unspecified/unknown cause from October 2022 onwards. Handling damage after smolt transfer was the 
primary cause of mortalities in June/July 2022 and other unspecified causes were the main cause in 
August and September 2022. Another driver for mortalities at the beginning of the production cycle was 
emaciated fish, commonly called “pinheads”. Severely emaciated fish are deemed to have poor welfare, 
with no or little potential for growth, and are therefore commonly removed from culture units and 
euthanised. While emaciation has been linked to diseases (e.g., McVicar, 1987; Roberts and Pearson, 
2005), it also occurs in fish absent of known bacterial or viral infections. There are also indications that 
it can be linked to stress during the hatchery phase or physiological problems during smoltification 
(Sommerset et al., 2022). As such, in this case, it may be a problem inherited from the freshwater 
hatchery and not related to any aspects of the Andfjord Salmon system or its operation. 
Establishing the cause behind the death of an individual fish can be operationally difficult. Dead fish may 
be in advanced states of decay, which makes discerning any signs of damage or disease impossible. 
Often, even in recently deceased or moribund fish, there are no clear external or internal signs that may 
reveal the cause and sophisticated diagnostic techniques can be costly and time consuming. That the 
proportion of mortalities with unknown or unspecified causes exceeds 10% is therefore not unexpected 
in a commercial setting. In a survey conducted among veterinary and fish health personnel in Northern 
Norway, mechanical damage associated with salmon lice is cited as the most important source of 
reduced welfare and mortalities, followed by Moritella viscosa and Tenacibaculum spp. bacterial 
infections (Sommerset et al., 2022). Audits of commercial production data from the region have also 
indicated that winter ulcer disease associated with Moritella viscosa is a considerable cause for 
mortalities and quality downgrading (Alvestad, 2021). During this first production cycle, none of these 
problems seem to have affected the Andfjord Salmon system to a considerable degree with mortalities 
remaining low through the winter and until harvest. Particularly, avoiding mechanical delousing 
treatments seems to contribute positively to fish welfare in the Andfjord Salmon system and considerably 
reduce overall mortality risk (Sviland Walde et al., 2021, Oliveira et al., 2021, Overton et al., 2018). 
However, more than 7% of the fish that were checked upon slaughter were downgraded due to the 
presence of small and mostly healed ulcers on the side of the fish (C. Thomassen, personal 
observations). As these were healed, they were unlikely to have occurred during or immediately before 
harvest or slaughter, yet there is no clear indication of a high prevalence of ulcers found in the welfare 
 
61 
 
scoring data. The aetiology of these small ulcers remains unknown but could have been related to scale 
loss and collisions between fish and the pool walls when fish jumped during surfacing activities, before 
floating air cushions were installed around the pool (C. Thomassen, pers. comm.). 
The daily feed delivery increased from stocking through the beginning of September and then decreased 
toward the end of 2022, along with declining temperature and daylengths. Following the decrease, the 
delivery remained stable around 0.5% for the remainder of the production cycle. Both the values and 
seasonal pattern correspond with those observed by Noble et al. (2007), who found that both 
temperature and the change in daylength significantly influenced the daily feed ration delivered by an 
on-demand feeding system. The stable and low delivery rate through the spring of 2023 may be due to 
the temperatures remaining low almost until harvest, not passing 7 °C before June. The dissolved 
oxygen saturation levels recorded during production in the Andfjord system far exceed the threshold for 
maximal feed intake for the relevant temperature range (Remen et al., 2016), so periods of reduced 
appetite do not appear to be related to oxygen levels. Whilst artificial lighting is used within the Andfjord 
salmon system, feed was generally administered during natural daylight or twilight hours. A short-term 
drop in appetite was observed following the onset of artificial lighting in November 2022 and turning it 
off in April 2023, and likely also following an incident where an ROV with mounted lamps scared the fish 
near the end of October 2022, possibly reflecting a short-term stress induced drop in appetite. Overall, 
however, in combination with the good growth and feed conversion rate observed, the recorded feed 
delivery should indicate a good appetite throughout – especially when considering the novelty of the 
system and its operation.  
Behaviour is a valuable, if complex OWI in salmonid aquaculture (Wiese et al., 2023) and how salmon 
behaviourally respond to their rearing environment and external stimuli can be an indicator of how they 
have adapted to the system they are reared in. A simple behavioural OWI toolbox was developed to 
document an array of behavioural OWIs, including: group structure; occurrences of clumping 
behaviours; the vertical and horizontal distribution and utilisation of the pool, and any occurrences of 
fleeing/panic behaviour. Appetite is also a valuable OWI (Stien et al., 2013) and staff impressions of 
daily appetite were also scored according to Laksvel (Nilsson et al., 2022) in addition to their impressions 
of daily surfacing activity.  
Numerous studies have demonstrated that salmon in aquacultural settings distribute themselves in 
relation to numerous environmental gradients (see e.g., Oppedal et al., 2011 and references therein). A 
major focus of the Andfjord Salmon welfare and health documentation plan has been to document a 
wide array of input-based environmental indicators within the pool in order to aid our understanding of 
the spatio-temporal effects of these OWIs upon the fish and the behaviours they express. During initial 
pool acclimation, farm staff observations noted that fish distributed themselves around the periphery of 
the pool (thigmotaxis) and distributed themselves in the upper layers of the water column during working 
hours, often seeking shade during the periods of direct sunlight in the first week following smolt transfer. 
Fish rapidly adapted to the pool and within weeks their horizontal and vertical distribution at high and 
low water observation periods was classified as stable and uniform. After pool acclimation, the fish 
generally avoided the periphery of the rearing system unless they were moribund. From early July 2022, 
fish exhibited a uniform and stable horizontal pool distribution, and this persisted until slaughter. Other 
studies have reported that in open cage systems fish generally avoid the cage periphery and base 
(Oppedal et al., 2011) and adopt a somewhat loose schooling group swimming structure. Loose 
schooling was the dominant group structure observed during behavioural inspections at low and high 
water and accounted for the majority of all observations. This is similar to the behaviour in cages 
reported by Oppedal et al., (2011). The remainder of observations mostly noted mixed behaviours. 
Incidences of large-scale uniform schooling behaviour were rare and loose schooling often involved 
smaller scale sub-groups of fish. This may be related to the generally low water velocities within the 
pool, as faster current speeds reportedly increase group structure and reduce structural variability (see 
 
62 
 
Solstorm et al., 2016 and also references therein; the experimental scale study by Solstorm et al., 2016, 
raised Atlantic salmon post-smolts ca.  100 g, in small raceways). Nilsen et al., (2019) noted that fish 
demonstrated mixed behaviours and a breakdown of schooling behaviours at low water velocities (ca. 2 
cm/s) in circular sea based semi-closed containment systems (S-CCS). However, this accounted for 
only up to ca. 15% of the observed behaviours in the Andfjord pool. Aside from isolated periods during 
the first two months of stocking, fish generally distributed themselves down to 15 – 20 m during 
observation periods at high and low water until the end of the production cycle. Johansson et al., (2014) 
also reported that post-smolts often distribute themselves throughout the water column in cage 
aquaculture at low water velocities. After underwater lighting was turned on, fish consistently distributed 
themselves down to 15 m in the water column for ca. 2 months, at both low and high water before again 
utilising the full depth of the pool until the end of the production cycle. The drivers of this are unclear, 
but it has been noted in other studies that fish are attracted to underwater light sources (Juell & 
Fosseidengen, 2004; Wright et al., 2019) and the application of underwater lighting may have led to the 
fish adapting their depth distribution in the pool. There were some anecdotal observations of fish seeking 
out potentially higher water velocities near the tank outlet in the month after pool stocking and this 
behaviour receded when the lights were turned on in the pool (C. Thomassen personal observations).  
Daily observations of the fish at both high and low water did not document dense clumping behaviour 
or fleeing/panic behaviour. However, there was an incident where there was a case of potential fish 
sensitivity to the ROV lighting in late October 2022. This led to short-term panic in some fish and a short-
term increase in associated mortalities. Andfjord Salmon staff removed the ROV from use until remedial 
action was taken (adjustments were made to the units so light intensity could be dimmed and that they 
could also turn them off when the daily operations were concluded) and this meant this unfortunate 
occurrence was short-lived and mitigated against. Surface jumping activity was generally low and stable 
in the pool until an increase in Caligus elongatus levels in August 2022 where it increased for one month 
before decreasing back to baseline levels (following in-feed slice treatment) for the remainder of the 
production period. Other studies have also reported increased surface jumping activity in relation to 
increase ectoparasitic pressure (e.g., Furevik et al, 1993; Samsing et al., 2015). 
 
6.1.3 
Individual level Outcome based OWIs  
The injury status of fish in the system was closely monitored during monthly manual audits of high 
numbers of fish in relation to the new Laksvel OWI scoring system (Nilsson et al., 2022). Individual injury 
based OWIs were documented ca. monthly for around 100 fish once before and for 5 months post-smolt 
transfer (T-1 to T5), before reducing the number of fish to 30 per sample until slaughter (primarily due 
to low water temperatures during winter and spring).  
Overall, the injury status of the fish has generally been good with the majority of injury based OWIs 
being scored as absent or mild according to the Laksvel scoring system (Nilsson et al., 2022). When 
OWI levels increase this was noted and followed. When considering first impression as an OWI in the 
injury toolbox, these impressions were scored worst ca. 30 and 60 days after pool transfer. These 
impressions were primarily driven by the emaciation state of some of the sampled fish, together with 
scale loss. A further OWI of interest was eye opacity which was worst ca. 90 days post seawater transfer 
followed by minor isolated periods where it increased in severity during winter and spring 2023. Other 
forms of eye damage were mostly scored as absent for >60% of fish with the rest mostly showing mild 
eye damage at most sampling points until mid-winter, and then the frequency of mild damage increased 
until slaughter. Eye opacity can be driven by a number of factors including diet, handling and the 
environment (e.g. Bjerkås et al., 2006) and is a common challenge in farming. The prevalence of 
moderate fin damage (to one or more fins) also increased as winter approached and was variable for 
the rest of the production cycle. The increased frequency of detected mild injuries as winter progressed 
might have also been driven by e.g., lower water temperatures impacting the healing response 
(Schneider and Nicholson, 1980; Jensen et al., 2015). As with the mortality OWI outlined above, the 
 
63 
 
temporal pattern of higher scale loss and potential emaciation problems associated with seawater 
transfer can be common, although histological skin health (see below) was worse than expected at this 
timepoint, although we must point out that this may be an artefact of sampling challenges. It should be 
noted that the Fish Health Service staff that carried out the OWI scoring also recorded that a portion of 
moribund “pinhead” fish were caught during the sampling procedure after 30 days, and the numbers of 
these fish were potentially higher in the sample than what was representative of the group as a whole. 
This is an inherent problem with the sampling procedure in all large-scale aquaculture production 
systems. 
With regard to other morphological indicators in the Laksvel toolbox: opercular damage was either 
absent or mild for the majority of the sampling points during the production cycle. The majority of fish 
had no or mild gill health problems for the majority of the production cycle aside from a limited numbers 
of sampled individuals in winter and spring 2023 that had moderate of severe gill status at limited 
sampling points. The frequency of moderate skin haemorrhaging was also generally low and varied 
during the production cycle. Vertebral and snout deformities, maturation, wounds and ulcers were mild 
or absent throughout the production cycle although the frequency of mild snout damage did often 
increase during the winter through to slaughter and as stated above, low water temperatures may have 
also impacted the healing response of fish at this time (Schneider and Nicholson, 1980; Jensen et al., 
2015).  
6.2 
Fish health monitoring  
Histological examination of skin and gills were carried out on monthly samples. External fish health 
services have reported good gill health during the production cycle in the Andfjord pilot pool. This 
correlates with the low number of gill health risks the fish have been exposed to. Various gill health risks 
can include mechanical delousing, high turbidity and poor water quality (see Noble et al., 2018, 
Sommerset et al., 2022 and references therein). Fish have not been exposed to these risks in the 
Andfjord system. During certain periods of the first production cycle in the Andfjord K0 pool, gill health 
was affected by pathogens and gas embolies. For example, from ca. six months after stocking (T6) until 
the end of the production cycle, any minor deviations in observed gill pathologies were related to 
pathogens and followed the PCR screening results. At timepoint T6 and T7 a low number of sampled 
fish (2 of 10 and 4 of 10 samples) demonstrated pathologies related to gas embolism in the gills. Gas 
embolisms can be driven by gas supersaturation, including DO values 110%-220% (Mesa et al., 2000; 
Espmark et al., 2010). The oxygen data suggests that the few individuals observed with gas embolies 
may have been located over time in the areas of the tank with higher oxygen saturation. The control 
measurements of total gas pressure also indicated some supersaturation toward the end of the 
production cycle. 
It has been reported that skin quality can be reduced after transfer to sea, and that damages induced 
during transfer can improve after 1-2 weeks (Karlsen et al., 2020). Due to this, Karlsen and colleagues 
suggested that the period after transfer to sea should be considered a barrier recovery phase, where 
the fish build resilience and robustness for further growth. Reduced skin quality is generally associated 
with a damaged epidermis, missing epidermis, scale loss or altered mucus production. This damage is 
often microscopic and impossible to detect during external examination of the whole fish during OWI 
scoring.   
Histological skin samples showed more epidermal damage than what is normally observed in other 
cases. Skin damage continued to be high through all sampling points. At T-1 to T3, these damages can 
reflect i.e., challenging sampling procedures, but after T4, the combination of challenging sampling, 
reduced temperatures and increased pathological findings can be the cause. Patogen AS (ref: screening 
results), also reported lost epidermis, inflammation and superficial damages in the majority of analyzed 
 
64 
 
individuals, with an increase from T6 and onwards. In response to these findings, project partners 
increased the frequency of histological skin samplings (in the original documentation plan skin samples 
were planned to be audited ca. every 3 – 6 months after T2 until slaughter). Interestingly, the amounts 
of macro-level scale loss (audited as a Laksvel OWI after Nilsson et al., 2022) were worst at sampling 
points T1 and T2 (ca. 30- and 60-days post-seawater transfer, respectively), before generally stabilising/ 
improving until T6. After T6, scale loss status as an OWI again improved until slaughter (see Figure 35). 
At T9-11, in the months prior to slaughter, the overall trend was for improved epidermis quality, with 
mucus cells more evenly distributed in the epidermis. A few samples also had big clusters of melanin.  
Nephrocalcinosis is seen as one of the biggest health challenges for salmon in hatcheries, with higher 
risk in RAS facilities due to aspects related to water quality (Sommerset et al., 2022). In the fish from 
the Andfjord system, the salmon developed Nephrocalcinosis in the hatchery phase before transfer to 
the Andfjord pool and we see the artefacts of this after their transfer to seawater and for the remainder 
of the production cycle. Nephrocalcinosis, when detected, was generally mild for the entire production 
cycle, but a small number of individuals were detected with a nephrocalcinosis score of level 4 at T4 
and there was also an increase in the numbers of fish with a level 2 score to >20% of the fish sampled. 
The reasons for this are unclear and these levels were not detected for the remainder of the study. 
6.3 
Production performance monitoring  
Fish growth, represented by the specific growth rate (SGR) and thermal growth coefficient (TGC), has 
been higher throughout the period than that indicated for fish in this weight and temperature range (4 to 
14 °C) by reference growth tables (Austreng et al., 1987). It is also in the upper range of values reported 
in several previous studies, overall. For the period in question, especially from late August until October 
2022, the reported values are above the range shown by previous studies (Dessen et al., 2017; 
Thorarensen and Farrell, 2011; Mørkøre and Rørvik, 2001). Einen et al. (1995) considered a well 
performing farmed Atlantic salmon to have a TGC around 3.3, overall. It is worth noting that previous 
studies have mostly been conducted at more southerly locations, experiencing longer days during the 
winter months, and in sea cages, experiencing different water velocities. The growth in the Andfjord 
salmon system is high also when compared with studies utilising semi-closed containment systems, 
although these studies are not easily comparable due to having been conducted during different 
seasons or using different sampling regimes (Nilsen et al., 2019, Balseiro et al., 2018). However, caution 
should be taken in comparing TGC levels between studies and production cycles, as the metric may not 
be temperature independent at very low or very high temperatures (Jobling, 2003). 
The standard deviation of the average weights is increasing slightly throughout the studied period but is 
lower than values observed for sea cage rearing in previous studies (e.g., Mørkøre and Rørvik, 2001; 
Roth et al., 2005). The values are likely representative of, or lower than, those normally experienced in 
fish populations not subjected to periodic weight grading, as in sea cage aquaculture. The condition 
factor rose from the start, reaching a peak in late September 2022 at 1.44, and then declined sharply 
towards the end of the year. A similar pattern has previously been observed in cage reared salmon 
released as one-year old smolts. Typically, Atlantic salmon in sea cage aquaculture tend to accumulate 
fat throughout the autumn (Dessen et al., 2017). This fat accumulation stagnates during the winter 
months, whereas body length growth will tend to continue, thereby reducing the condition factor 
(Mørkøre and Rørvik, 2001). Good growth conditions may facilitate the onset of maturation in Atlantic 
salmon under culture conditions (e.g., Good and Davison, 2016). However, neither the welfare scoring 
data, nor the slaughter data indicated a concerning level of sexual maturation in the fish during this 
production cycle. Whether prolonging the production cycle through the summer would have increased 
the incidence or risk of sexual maturation is not possible to determine at this stage. 
 
65 
 
The overall FCR is comparable to or lower than that seen in studies utilising traditional sea cage rearing, 
where an increase in FCR is commonly seen towards the end of the year. For the period between early 
September until early January, Mørkøre and Rørvik (2001) observed an FCR above 2.0. The stock was 
harvested before the stocking biomass density reached 25 kg/m3. Whilst stocking density is subject to 
many confounding factors and not necessarily in itself a sufficient determinant of poor welfare, at least 
within reasonable limits, there are indications that densities from 50 kg/m3 and up affect skin health in 
Atlantic salmon post-smolt (Sveen et al., 2016). There is presently little to indicate that increasing the 
biomass density in the Andfjord system beyond 25 kg/m3 will increase the risk of adverse fish welfare 
states, if good water quality and rearing practices are otherwise maintained. 
The levels of salmon louse (Lepeophtheirus salmonis) observed were negligible. There was, however, 
a noticeable level of Caligus elongatus observed, reaching over 0.5 lice per fish on average in 
September 2022, albeit low compared with observed levels in a previous study from UK cage farming 
of Atlantic salmon (Revie et al, 2002). Caligus elongatus is known to cause less severe damage to the 
skin of Atlantic salmon, compared with Lepeophtheirus salmonis, however with some risk of secondary 
infections and reduced welfare. Whilst the Andfjord salmon systems seawater intake is at 40 meters 
depth, the vertical distribution of Caligus elongatus may exceed this. The swimming capacity of Caligus 
elongatus is generally better than that of Lepeophtheirus salmonis, and the parasite can easily move 
between hosts. Little is known about the spatial distribution and temporal dynamics of this species (á 
Norði et al., 2015). Chemical treatments, e.g., as administered through feed, are effective against 
Caligus elongatus (Sommerset et al., 2022, Hemmingsen et al., 2020).

7 Concluding remarks 
As stated earlier, in many cases broad knowledge on selecting and applying appropriate, fit for purpose 
OWI and LABWI toolboxes for documenting fish welfare and health in many new and emerging 
aquaculture systems is currently lacking. An aim of the Andfjord Salmon documentation project was 
therefore to use a wide-ranging and comprehensive suite of OWIs/LABWIs to document the possible 
effects of the rearing system and its operations upon aspects of the welfare and health of the fish, and 
Andfjord Salmon applied a very comprehensive welfare and health documentation plan for the first 
production cycle. This final report documents aspects of the welfare and health of the fish for the entire 
production cycle until slaughter, using a suite of input and outcome-based welfare indicators, in addition 
to a number of additional health indicators. 
Overall, the welfare and health of fish in the system was good in relation to e.g., the Laksvel scoring 
scheme for input- and outcome-based indicators for salmon. A wide range of environmental OWIs and 
LABWIs documented mostly uniform high levels of key water quality parameters such as oxygen 
throughout the pool. Water current speeds, whilst low, did not manifest in poor water quality and may 
have been increased by the presence of fish in the system. Minor differences in measured water quality 
parameters were found at different locations in the system but should not have consequence for fish 
welfare and should allow for uniform distribution of fish in the system. As water is drawn from depth 
before being pumped into the system, the water temperature was somewhat more stable than what 
would be expected at corresponding depths in open cages and followed seasonal patterns in ambient 
water temperature. Although oxygen was added to the inlet water, an effect of water temperature and 
increased biomass on dissolved oxygen saturation was observed during most intensive parts of the 
production. The low natural water temperatures the fish have been subjected to over the autumn through 
spring did not manifest in any welfare or performance risks, or risks for e.g., winter ulcers. 
Mortalities, excluding the sampling of fish without signs of disease, were very low in the rearing system 
(ca. 2.5% for the entire documentation period) and any minor mortality events were closely monitored 
and remedial actions, where possible to identify and apply, were taken. Appetite was generally good 
throughout the production cycle and growth performance and condition factor were also good. Fish 
adapted to the rearing system and generally utilised the pool in a uniform manner. No fleeing/panic 
behaviours were observed at observation times around high and low water, and where potential fish 
sensitivity to ROV lighting in late October 2022 led to short-term panic in some fish and a short-term 
increase in associated mortalities, remedial action was taken to ensure this was short-lived and 
corrected for. 
The injury status of fish in the system was closely monitored during circa monthly manual audits of either 
i) 100 fish (until 12th December 2022) or ii) 30 fish (numbers of sampled fish were reduced due to low 
winter temperatures from 26th January 2023 until 21st June 2023). The injury status of fish in the system 
was closely monitored during monthly manual audits of either i) 100 fish (until 12th December 2022) or 
ii) 30 fish (numbers of sampled fish were reduced due to low winter temperatures from 26th January 
2023 until 21st June 2023). The injury status of fish in relation to the Laksvel Operational Welfare 
Indicator (OWI) scoring system (Nilsson et al., 2022) has generally been good and the majority of injury 
based OWIs were scored as absent or mild for most of the production cycle. When OWI levels increased, 
this was noted, and causes investigated. After smolt transfer several fish were sampled with poorer 
condition (emaciation state) and scale loss (<20% of fish sampled) but this injury status improved 90 
days after stocking. 
Fish Health Services took circa monthly samples (minimum 10 fish per sample) from the heart, kidney, 
gill, skin, liver, spleen, pseudo branch, pancreas, pyloric caeca and skeletal muscle (full organ pack, 
routine assessment) for histological examination to evaluate the integrity and quality of these organs. 
 
67 
 
Skin samples were also taken for histological evaluation by Nofima. Histological evaluation of gill health 
and kidney status from the full organ pack assessment was considered to have high clinical importance 
and is summarized here with kind permission from Fish Health Services and Andfjord Salmon AS. Heart 
status is summarized based upon PCR data. Gill health was generally good at all timepoints, except for 
an increase in infectious pathogens that were the main cause of pathological findings from T6 to 
slaughter. Whereas skin got a higher score (more damaged) from the hatchery phase up until 60 days 
post-transfer to the pool. This skin health histology score was often higher than what was expected 
based upon the OWI scoring. However, it is not clear if this was due to reduced skin quality per se, poor 
sample quality, or to challenges and limitations with the sampling procedures. Histological findings in 
skin were comparable to the welfare score at the start of the documentation period and included 
superficial damages such as lost epidermis and scale loss over the same time points, in the majority of 
analysed individuals. Based on these findings, skin morphology and skin health were followed more 
closely for the remainder of the production cycle. Fish developed Nephrocalcinosis in the hatchery phase 
before transfer to the Andfjord pool and we see the artefacts of this after their transfer to seawater. With 
regard to PCR based pathogen screening, 80-100% of sampled fish from T0-T11 were categorized with 
suspected heart- and skeletal muscle inflammation (HSMI), with positive results for Piscine 
orthoreovirus 1 (PRV-1). At T4 and T5, 3% of sampled fish were positive for the parasite 
Paranucleospora theridion and at T6 43% of sampled fish were positive for the parasite Parvicapsula 
pseudobranchicola and 73% of the sampled fish positive for the bacteria Candidatus Branchiomonas 
cysticola. At timepoint T8, 17% of the samples were positive for the parasite Ichthyobodo salmonis. 
These pathogens persisted to the end of the production cycle but had no clinical impact on the overall 
health situation in the pool, which was classified by fish health services as either good or not requiring 
attention at all sampling points. The results for all other pathogen screenings were negative. 
Whilst only a negligible amount of Lepeophtheirus salmonis was observed in the system, there was a 
noticeable but relatively low abundance of Caligus elongatus during the late summer/autumn of 2022. 
This did not pose a major risk to fish health or welfare and was easily remedied by feed administered 
treatments. No Caligus elongatus were found in 2023. Infestation levels should nevertheless be 
monitored closely for future production cycles.
